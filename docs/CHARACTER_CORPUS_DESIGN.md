# A diverse cast beyond the first eight

Status: DEFERRED after the user's scope correction. This records future ideas, not an active character-creator project. See CHARACTER_SCOPE_OPTIONS.md. Internal reuse and an optional preset selector are distinct from arbitrary creature construction.

24 September 2026. The user wants a large, varied design corpus: mammals, canine/feline influences, apes, birds, reptiles, fish and stranger forms of life. The eight-philosophy sheet is a starting exploration, not eight final species or a cap. User specifically suggested a fish in a floating orb.

## Separate anatomy, civilization and individual

A species is not a philosophy, occupation, nation or moral alignment. One aquatic species can contain several nations; individuals can be merchants, artists, scientists or soldiers. An empire's institutions and philosophy influence behavior without prescribing one body type or a universal personality. Avoid equating apes with warfare, insects with evil or mammals with friendliness.

Use four independently authored layers:

1. **Body plan and habitat:** proportions, senses, manipulation, movement, life support, expressive anatomy.
2. **Culture and material life:** clothing fitted to the body, tools, cuisine, family structures, vehicles, architecture and personal objects.
3. **Government and philosophy:** separate institutions and priorities; map them to actual decisions and obligations.
4. **Individual:** occupation, desire, fear, personal bond, contradiction, voice and relationship to the player.

Reuse approved anatomy/rigs where sensible, then create meaningful individual and cultural differences. Do not market simple recolors as distinct species. Do not randomly combine incompatible limbs and outfits and call that authored diversity.

## Exploration families

| Body-plan influence | Visual/expression opportunities | Production question before approval |
|---|---|---|
| Feline/canine mammals | Different muzzles, mobile ears, whiskers, scent attention and body proportions | How alien is the anatomy beyond a familiar Earth animal wearing clothes? Can short fur be conveyed by geometry/materials at portrait scale? |
| Ape-like mammals | Long arms, shoulder mass, deliberate hands, posture and gaze | Keep a genuinely distinct alien anatomy and individual temperament; avoid one generic gorilla soldier. |
| Other mammals | Hooves, trunks, digging forelimbs, aquatic forms and varied sensory organs | How do hands/tools/clothes adapt? Do not default every body to a human torso. |
| Birds | Beaks, crests, feather fans, head movements and wing gestures | Define manipulation: feet, adapted wing digits, separate manipulators or assistive devices; feather cards/meshes need bounded cost. |
| Reptiles | Scales, frills, shells, coiling bodies and lateral eyes | Derive eye/neck/limb movement and seating from actual anatomy. Fangs are optional, not a villain marker. |
| Amphibious forms | Soft skin, gills, webbed hands, changing posture between environments | Define moisture/habitat needs and visual expression without simulating every physiological process. |
| Fish/rays/eels | Fin signaling, floating/swimming posture, unusual eyes and aquatic vehicles | Habitat and external manipulators must make interaction believable; animated water is a visual effect, not fluid simulation. |
| Cephalopods/gastropods | Grasping arms, mantle shape, beaks, skin signals and low bodies | Lock attachment map and deformation before making a rig; our merchant is the pilot, not the template for every species. |
| Arthropods | Segmentation, multiple limbs, feelers and compound eyes | Use intentionally among other forms; first sheet overused related shapes. |
| Radial/colonial/symbiotic organisms | Multiple attention centers, unfamiliar communication, partners with different roles | Establish identity, agency and manipulators clearly; one strong readable idea before detailed lore. |

These are design influences, not claims about actual extraterrestrial taxonomy or a request to implement all life-support systems now.

## Fish in a floating orb

SUPERSEDED: the user rejects the elaborate vehicle/tools described below. Latest direction is simply an ordinary fish in a plain floating round orb, no tools or machinery. The apparent harmlessness contrasting with possible great power is the appeal. See PLAIN_FISH_AND_ASSET_VERSIONS.md. The earlier apparatus proposal remains historical, not a production requirement.

Proposed character: an experienced aquatic broker or port official who uses a transparent water-filled mobility sphere. It is ordinary transport and habitat for this person, not a novelty pet bowl. A small external stabilizer ring carries one articulated gripper, communications hardware and a docking socket. The fish faces the visitor, hovers closer to inspect an offered sample, and operates internal controls with fins or body gestures. The gripper handles solid wares outside.

Personal history could appear through a family token on the ring, a repair patch and instruments collected from different ports. Leave personality open: a formal bureaucrat, confident merchant or impulsive explorer all work. A fish need not be the joke. The surprising vehicle and purposeful behavior already provide charm.

Performance spec: deliberate turn-to-face, attentive hovering, fin emphasis, back-away refusal and a gripper exchange. A few bubbles and controlled water/caustic cues suggest the medium. Implementation would use capped particles and a tested shell shader or simplified material; no real-time volumetric fluid simulation. Refraction/transparent sorting on the current renderer must be prototyped before promising the exact orb appearance. Do not let a perfect sphere hide the fish's readable expression.

## Corpus production loop

1. Author small batches, initially six to eight genuinely different silhouette/behavior cards. Each card has a distinct physical idea, personality intention and clear interaction role.
2. Review candidates at both large size and actual contact-portrait size. Track liked directions, rejections and unresolved details separately.
3. For a selected candidate, lock anatomy and generate/reconcile front, side, back and expression references. Contradictory AI views are corrected before modeling.
4. Produce one real mesh and engine turntable. Shape/appearance must pass before multiplying costumes, texture variants or related species.
5. Finish deformation topology, materials and a few authored behaviors. Test in contact/trade with the same persistent actor.
6. Expand only proven families: distinct ages/proportions, occupations, personal objects, clothing, voices and cultural variants. Reuse work without erasing individuality.

Each catalog record needs ID, parent body family, spec/prompt provenance, reference, approval status, anatomy notes, palette/materials, movement/communication method, production source, mesh/rig/texture versions, measured budgets and actual captures. Concept-ready, mesh-ready, animated, integrated and user-accepted are separate statuses. None of this requires per-citizen simulation or a creature editor.

The user's desired close visual match (approximately 95%) remains the target. Current merchant prototypes do not meet it. Larger concept volume must not be misreported as progress toward finished 3D fidelity.
