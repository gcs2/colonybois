# Visible planetary production, scarcity and unlocks

> Design intent; task status and execution order live in the task board. [Documentation map](../README.md).

User direction, 23 September 2026. This is tracked for the post-MVP expansion of the core exploration/trade loop. Do not divert the current energy/HUD correction into farming production.

## What the player should see and do

Discover visible deposits, mines, extraction installations, cultivated plants and fruit, animal farms, and material culture made by different civilizations. Harvesting and extraction must have effective tools and logistics. Expensive development should create useful sustained output, capacity or capabilities after a credible payback period. Infrastructure is physically visible on the actual planet, with development and operating states.

Value depends on the buyer and its needs, not a universal rarity-to-price ladder. Ordinary Earth wood could be a useful and sought-after alien import because of its structural, acoustic, biological or cultural uses. Cultural artifacts should retain their civilization, material and provenance; not every collectible is ancient technology.

## Content composition

Build a wide corpus across minerals, industrial materials, plants, fruit, animals and manufactured/cultural goods. The bulk consists of ordinary items with normal parameters and **no random modifiers**. Common does not mean economically useless: reliable bulk supply supports production and trade.

Rare, epic or legendary discoveries may have authored distinctive traits. Those traits must enable a real recipe, environmental interaction, equipment capability, husbandry constraint or buyer preference. Color/name/stat inflation does not count as unique content. Rarity labels are an available organization, not a requirement to turn every resource into loot with affixes.

Every unusual item or organism needs a scarcity rule: limited deposits, habitat requirements, recovery times, breeding limits, seasonal yield, finite buyer demand, transport requirements or restricted cultural provenance. Avoid unlimited duplication of rare properties through trivial breeding, recycling or save/loading. Do not impose every constraint on every common item.

For each content entry, track: family, ordinary/exceptional status, origin, standard physical/economic parameters, authored trait if any, resource source and replenishment, harvesting/extraction method, capital/operating needs, output, logistics constraints, buyers/recipes, art/animation/audio requirements, and implementation/review evidence.

## Investment and simulation

Model output and stocks in aggregate. A mine consumes power and logistics capacity; farms depend on suitability and inputs; extraction can deplete stocks or create environmental effects. Capped machines, workers, animals and cargo movements illustrate aggregate flows. No world-scale organism or citizen simulation.

Compare capital cost, upkeep, time to first output, route capacity, finite demand and expected payback. A remote high-value source should present an investment decision, not automatic profit. Published prices and projected output must make that decision understandable.

## Achievements, badges and shops

Achievements and badges unlock real technologies, equipment and new shop inventory. Recognition should create a new option. Unlocking stock does **not** automatically grant the item: purchase price, energy, installation and prerequisites still matter. Show the required badge, progress, newly available stock, provider and price, then record the unlock in the chronicle.

Use distinct accomplishments in exploration, diplomacy, trade, intervention and combat. Provide alternative advancement paths so essential tools do not require repetitive quotas or forced combat. Later rare-item traits can open specialized badge paths, but foundational navigation, basic tools and access to recovery remain available from the start.

Persist badge evidence, unlock IDs and shop stock separately from UI state. Reopening a shop or reloading a save must not duplicate rewards. The existing prototype milestone text is not yet this integrated progression system.

## Acceptance when scheduled

A player identifies a resource from its surface site, understands its value to a particular buyer, invests in production, sees the installation change, and eventually earns enough useful output to justify the expense. A rare discovery changes what the player can do and remains scarce. A badge unlocks a visible, purchasable technology in a real shop. Common materials remain the bulk of the economy.
