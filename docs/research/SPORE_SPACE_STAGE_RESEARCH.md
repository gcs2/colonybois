# Spore Space Stage: preserve the play

> Reference research; observations, proposals and evidence gaps are not implementation status. [Documentation map](../README.md).

Research checkpoint: 2026-09-22. The user wants the fun of Spore's Space Stage expanded, including richer worlds, trade, diplomacy and history. Those ideas remain endorsed. This document corrects an overly abstract management emphasis; it does not cancel the space-first direction or approve every proposed feature below.

## What the reference actually offers

These are paraphrased mechanics from the linked sources. The design lessons are our interpretation. Several Fandom pages blocked direct retrieval; indexed article extracts were available, including substantial sections. No hands-on Spore playtest was performed for this review. Fan-fiction pages and unverified discussion-page claims are excluded. Base Space Stage and the Galactic Adventures expansion must remain distinct.

| System | Reference mechanics | Design lesson for Frontier Worlds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal ship and tools | The ship collects plants, animals and artifacts with an abduction beam. Taking another civilization's citizens or property can worsen relations. [Abduction Beam](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Abduction_Beam) | A discovery should be something you can approach, inspect and act on. The same tool can support science, rescue or theft, with different consequences. |
| World transformation | Atmosphere and temperature determine T-score; ecological slots support plants and animals. A T1 ecosystem accommodates three plant sizes, two herbivores and a predator. Higher tiers support more life and colony development. Tools can change climate through energy use or consumable charges. [Terraforming](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Terraforming) | Collecting life and transforming worlds connect exploration to development. Show the change happening. Our ecology can diverge from the fixed slot recipe without becoming an organism-by-organism simulation. |
| Trading personally | Different markets offer different spice prices. Producers can sell cheaply; players can find better buyers elsewhere. Own colonies supply collected cargo, and cargo capacity constrains accumulation. [Spice](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Spice) | Finding a profitable destination is satisfying discovery. Keep optional personal trading and the first expedition's payoff. Automate an established business, not the discovery of the opportunity. |
| Diplomatic trade routes | Space-stage routes provide access to partner spice and advance an economic takeover meter toward purchasing a system. They do not directly pay Sporebucks. [Trade Route](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Trade_Route) | Trade already offers an alternative expansion path. Our recurring deliveries and real supply chains are an extension, not a description of how Spore's routes work. Economic influence should open new actions. |
| Civilizational intervention | A monolith can advance a world's inhabitants toward spaceflight, producing a new empire with a favorable relationship toward its uplifter. [Monolith](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Monolith) | The galaxy can acquire new actors because of something the player did. A world should remain interesting after its first survey. Uplift is a later candidate, not required for our first slice. |
| Philosophy and powers | Archetypes connect prior play to special abilities and affect diplomatic relationships. Most have a unique power; Wanderer does not. [Philosophy](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Philosophy) | Divergence should change the actions available to the player. Government and philosophy remain separate in our game; neither should be merely a passive percentage modifier. |
| Tool progression | Tool unlocks can offer alternative badge requirements. [Tools reference](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Spore/Tools) | Keep anticipation of a new capability and several ways to earn it. Avoid forcing one repeated activity as the only path to an essential tool. |
| Allies and fighting | Allied ships can accompany the flagship; losing them carries diplomatic consequences. Allied empires can also be asked to attack a planet. [Space fleets](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Space_fleets) | Relationships should become visible help in the world. Later combat should preserve personal participation and recognizable allies, as well as strategic orders. |
| Expedition geography | Approaching the galactic core reduces travel range; reaching it involves hostile territory, preparation and route choices. [Galactic Core](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Core) | Exploration needs anticipation, danger and distant goals. Fog of war alone is not enough. Equipment and friendships can change where you dare to go. |
| Discovery and collection | Strange natural landmarks and ancient-looking structures decorate planets; the game also includes collectible artifacts and unusual worlds. [Natural landmarks](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Natural_landmarks), [Galactic Core's linked discovery categories](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Galactic_Core) | Some discoveries can simply be delightful, eerie or worth collecting. Not everything needs a production recipe or a moral dilemma. |
| History | Spore already presents a species-history timeline and connects earlier decisions to consequence abilities. [History](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/History) | Our chronicle develops an existing part of the inspiration. It should preserve the player's particular civilization, including decisions, treaties and losses. |

The important combination is personal agency at several scales: a creature in your cargo hold, a planet you changed, an alien who remembers you, and a larger region you can now reach. A map with resource nodes cannot deliver that by itself.

## Separate interesting friction from repetition

These are design judgments to test, not claims that every Spore player dislikes the same things.

| Preserve | Reduce or replace |
| --- | --- |
| Discovering a buyer, deciding what to carry, making an early profitable sale | Repeating an identical safe collection/sale circuit indefinitely to fund essential progress |
| Collecting a strange organism and seeing it establish itself somewhere else | Rebuilding the same ecological checklist with the same specimens on every world |
| Responding personally to a meaningful emergency | Repeated compulsory returns home that interrupt an expedition without an interesting choice |
| Threats, opportunistic enemies and consequences for neglected commitments | Making the flagship the only competent emergency service in a growing empire |
| Earning tools that change what you can do | Progression that demands a large quota of substantially identical errands |
| Expressive alien contact and relationship maintenance | Repetitive dialogue clicks that offer no new information, leverage or decision |

Spore's ecological disasters illustrate the distinction: an infected species can lead to ecological collapse, but the response involves hunting marked infected creatures under a timer. Colony tools already mitigate these incidents: Bio Protectors slow their progression; Bio Stabilizers reduce a planet's likelihood of being targeted. We should recognize those existing mitigations rather than claiming Spore offers none. [Eco Disaster](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Eco_Disaster), [Bio Stabilizer](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Bio_Stabilizer)

For our game, delegated protection should be useful early and consume real capacity. A prepared settlement handles ordinary trouble; an unusual threat can still justify a personal visit. Warnings should show stakes and available responses. Peaceful play should retain uncertainty and opportunity.

## How the endorsed ideas fit

**Trade expands an adventure the player started.** Personally discovering a resource, testing a use and meeting a buyer creates the business. A recurring agreement then makes it dependable. Finite demand and route capacity support choices about new destinations; they should not bury every purchase in negotiation or administration.

**Living planets invite experiments.** A scanner reveals actionable behavior, a sampler acquires something tangible, and a deployment tool lets the player try an intervention. A few ecological relationships can support several uses. Species can also be amusing companions or collection subjects; not every animal exists to become a commodity.

**Politics is encountered through characters and capabilities.** A partner might offer access to a specialized tool, a safe harbor or an escort. An offended nation may restrict access to a resource you actually use. Keep expressive contact screens and visible reasoning. Nations, species and planets remain distinct.

**The ancestors' mystery is present in what works.** An ancient machine responding to a living organism can change what the player does next. Another society may still use it. The campaign can frame these encounters without making the sandbox wait for a chain of recordings. This is an encounter pattern, not a newly approved historical explanation.

**Art includes responses.** A fauna specification needs movement, scan response, collection/deployment behavior, silhouette, sound and scale alongside the mesh. A ship specification needs readable tools and cargo feedback. A beautiful static planet is only part of the asset pipeline.

## Proposed first playable interaction slice

Preserve the existing game. Add an isolated, deliberately small planet encounter before widening the economy or building full fleet combat:

1. Approach one authored world with a maneuverable scout in an accessible external view. Test responsive movement, zoom, tool targeting and audio. Keep the planned first-person feasibility experiment bounded and separate; cockpit combat is not a prerequisite for this encounter.
2. Locate an unusual moving creature and a resource-bearing plant. Scan them, receive one useful clue, and collect a sample with visible feedback. Give the creature a memorable nonessential reaction too.
3. Apply one bounded environmental intervention and deploy the sample in a suitable patch. Show a visible change and a persistent ecological/resource outcome. No dynamic planet-wide terrain deformation required.
4. Contact one expressive alien representative. Choose between keeping the discovery, making a first sale or proposing recurring access. Establishing a route must leave the player free to explore again.
5. Return later to see the outcome; the chronicle records the actual action and consequence. One new tool or access opportunity should make a previously noticed feature newly usable.

An illustrative encounter: floating grazers gather around charged seed pods. Scanning explains the relationship; a collected pod can help cultivate a resource on another suitable world. Taking the last viable patch harms the local population, while limited sampling leaves it healthy. A neighboring culture offers equipment for cultivated supply. Exact organisms, recipes and lore remain proposals. Do not require simulating every seed or animal.

Acceptance: the player voluntarily spends time flying, trying a tool, watching a reaction or revisiting the world. They can explain what changed and why. The encounter should be enjoyable before it feeds a six-good supply chain. Test this through actual input and human review, not just state assertions.

Keep the three-world economy, diplomatic UI, history, eventual fleet flight and authored-art pipeline. This is an additional gameplay foundation, not a replacement vision. Uplift, planet destruction, a large tool catalog, creature creation and seamless travel remain outside the initial slice.
