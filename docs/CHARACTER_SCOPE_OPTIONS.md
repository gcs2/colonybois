## Internal reuse does not require a creature editor

Recommended production structure: authored species and named characters, with shared source tools, materials, eye/gaze components, props, clothes and rigs where anatomy is compatible. Body plans remain deliberate. Avoid a universal interchangeable limb system merely to save work; making arbitrary anatomy deform and animate can cost more than separate controlled rigs.

| Option | Player experience | Cost and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Authored characters | A curated cast; player starts with an authored identity or chooses a prepared portrait | Smallest scope, strongest art control; recommended for current game |
| Preset selector later | Choose among finished species/portraits, several approved face variants and outfits; perhaps background | Bounded customization, compatible with internal reuse; recommended if player identity becomes important |
| Constrained sliders later | A few proportions within a single prepared anatomy | Requires deformation, fit and expression testing for every allowed range; more expensive than presets |
| Freeform creature construction | Place limbs, alter body topology, resize arbitrary parts | Large independent product; remains excluded/deferred, not implied by asset reuse |

“Skyrim-like selector” here means the general concept of choosing prepared appearance options, not copying Skyrim assets or interface. Original assets and presentation remain required. Under U.S. copyright guidance, ideas/systems/methods are distinguished from their protected expression; a modular editor is not automatically infringement simply because another game has one. ## Latest art correction

The v2 philosophy sheet looks too much like Disney-style animated animal characters to the user: large glossy eyes, familiar Earth animal heads, polished fur and humanized appealing expressions. The user also finds Prosperity and Harmony too similar. Preserve their earlier liked features as references, but do not treat two related purple tentacle designs as sufficient cast diversity.

The eight philosophy names are personality/behavior guides, not a demand for fixed species assignments. The merchant remains the first character concept; Harmony can use a wholly different body family. Proposed strongest differentiation: an aquatic character in a floating habitat, motivated by the user's fish-in-orb idea. This is a concept option, not a new implemented species or a mandate to replace all Harmony characters.

| Direction | Character treatment | Production tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Tactile miniature (recommended) | Matte sculpted surfaces, deliberate imperfections, varied eye size/placement, restrained acting and warm physical props | Readable forms and bounded materials; still needs good modeling and animation, not a guarantee of easy fidelity |
| Graphic alien | Bold silhouettes, controlled exaggerated anatomy, simplified material detail and strong shape/color grouping | Potentially economical and readable; must avoid looking like temporary primitives or flat icons |
| Strange naturalism | Biological surfaces and unusual posture, less human expression, quieter color | Convincing strangeness; risk of coldness, horror or heavy texture/rig work |

Keep identical merchant/aquatic identities across a comparison so rendering language can be judged separately from anatomy. No giant glassy eyes, human eyebrows, eyelashes or smiles added merely to signal friendliness. Appeal can come from attention, gesture, curiosity, confidence and purposeful behavior.
