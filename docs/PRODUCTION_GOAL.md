# Frontier Worlds production goal

Product objective refreshed 25 September 2026, without a token budget. This file defines what success means; it is not a live app-goal status report or a second work queue. The [task board](TASK_BOARD.md) owns current ordering, the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) owns milestone gates, and the [handoff](NEXT_SESSION.md) owns the latest checkpoint. Character production is deferred; the overall objective remains broader than any one milestone.

## Product objective

Build Frontier Worlds into a polished, playable, replayable Windows single-player spiritual successor to Spore's Space Stage in Godot 4.7.2. The player personally flies a ship through a vibrant galaxy, explores living worlds, meets expressive civilizations, gathers and trades resources, develops useful equipment and colonies, faces consequential danger, and builds a memorable campaign history. Prioritize satisfying connected play over feature counts or isolated prototypes.

Use [TASK_BOARD.md](TASK_BOARD.md) as the sole production queue and canonical feature ledger. Maintain a clear inventory of agreed Space Stage systems and meaningful views/states, then pursue the agreed baseline before a separate content-enrichment epic. Respect explicit scope boundaries: no creature creator, no coloring or terrain sculpting, further planet modification deferred, and no compulsory gopher missions. Cities support the space game through aggregate simulation. Detailed factory management, on-foot play, full fleet battles, skill trees, and engine migration require their own playability and scope gates; they are not implied by a resource-production loop.

Use Spore research, the source-to-runtime coverage register, and approved visual canon to guide implementation. Keep a mock/coverage inventory for every view and meaningful state, including normal, populated, empty, locked, unaffordable, invalid, combat, communication, reward, and save/load states. Label proposals and deferred screens. Create a task-backed designer brief when an implementation lacks visual direction, using the tracked [visual canon home](../art/visual-canon/README.md) and [mock-request home](art/mock-requests/README.md). Continue source study with timestamped observations and before/during/after evidence, cross-check the manual, and label inference. This evidence work informs active implementation; a complete mock atlas or another paused-frame audit is not a blanket prerequisite to the next connected playable slice. Close source and view coverage before claiming the agreed Space Stage baseline.

Build from the user-approved targets in [art/visual-canon](../art/visual-canon/README.md): the selected Morrow surface, orbital, system-map and approach views, plus the earlier galaxy view as a supporting reference. Ordinary flight uses a practical player-follow camera; cinematic framing is a later optional mode. Preserve broad world-to-scout scale, the pictorial right-side inventory and real quantities, top-left notifications, top-right Marks, compact hull/energy, and secondary altitude. Surface, system, and galaxy navigation each use their own full-screen composition; the local terrain chart appears only on a planet surface. Galaxy range is shown in its plane through full camera orbit. Use the selected Field Instruments language: matte ivory housings, charcoal insets, legible typography, recognizable pictorial controls, restrained functional colors, named tooltips, clear interaction states, and 1080p+ support. Assembly animation should feel physical and playful; boarding stays skippable, routine controls immediate, and reduced motion supported.

Deliver one persistent campaign loop with responsive mouse-first, player-following flight; optional arrows/numpad; clear ascent, descent and scale navigation; discovery and range progression; inhabited worlds and expressive contact; finite trade; consequences, danger, readable combat, shops, equipment, colonies, and satisfying achievements. Energy never regenerates passively: use inventory supplies or docking; homeworld recharge is free and other service prices are visible. Mining and harvesting must eventually connect to processing and useful manufactured items that improve a ship, expedition, or colony or can be traded. Inputs, capacity, energy, time, operating cost, demand, access, and outcomes must be real and persistent. A recipe list without useful effects is not a production loop; automate established repetition without removing discovery. Creature kills may yield resources but must not create mandatory grinding.

The world experience must grow beyond the current bounded basin: the same seeded landforms and coastlines must agree between globe and ground, regions must connect through traversable flight, and revisits must preserve meaningful changes. Morrow must read as inhabited through visible city and port activity, useful services, destinations, and clear communications. Stream detail around the player and keep population, traffic, memory, and frame-time costs bounded through aggregate simulation and measured budgets.

The agreed Space Stage baseline is more than a feature inventory: the campaign must support at least two distinct, viable approaches whose choices change routes, resources, relationships, rewards, or obligations in persistent history. Preserve the forgotten-outpost/Vanguard/ancestors campaign and morally complex alien politics as the later story and content arc. A human Sol start is a separate desired scenario, not a replacement for the alien campaign; its implementation and pacing remain gated.

## Acceptance criteria

- Across M1-M3, every implemented view and meaningful state must retain a source/canon/runtime trace, same-state evidence, and separate independent-critic and user verdicts as required by the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md). During development, use targeted review for material changes; a pending verdict blocks that acceptance gate, not independent reversible work. Never infer acceptance from a mock or silence.
- Before passing or scaling a relevant gate, define and record a performance budget on named target hardware. Set any numeric threshold from measurement; this goal does not invent one.
- Complete a bounded accessibility and input review covering legibility at supported resolutions, visible keyboard focus, mouse and left-handed keyboard paths, saved remapping, reduced motion, and visual or caption equivalents for essential audio/VO.
- Listen to an integrated build for responsive flight/combat/UI effects, ambience, music, and communications. Essential guidance and speech must be captioned; placeholder audio is not a quality pass.
- Before M1 closes, smoke-launch a clean Windows export and verify that it can save and reload the milestone voyage. Recheck save migrations and campaign continuity at later integration gates.
- Keep the project portable and reviewable from its canonical Git repository: approved visual canon, mock briefs/prompts, source provenance, and required implementation assets must live in documented, tracked homes. A clean checkout must reproduce the documented import/build/run path without ignored files from an agent worktree. Evidence and handoffs point to stable repository paths or clearly label local-only captures.
- Keep validation proportional to risk: run the narrowest relevant domain checks, record elapsed cost, skip unchanged suites, and run the full suite at a major integration gate or by explicit request. Tests establish only what they exercise; they do not prove fun, visual acceptance, performance, or native input feel.

Resolve major playability and presentation gaps before adding breadth. Verify deterministic simulation, persistence, costs, and access rules alongside the criteria above. Native playability, audio, human visual review, and user acceptance remain separate gates. Tests and attractive mocks alone do not establish completion.

Specify reusable original assets and audio before multiplying content; get concrete price/license approval before purchases. Retain sandbox play and story unlocks without compulsory grind. Read [SYNTHETIC_SQUAD.md](delivery/SYNTHETIC_SQUAD.md) for the Sol-led, isolated-worktree delivery model; it may speed independent work but does not add a queue or change acceptance authority.

Report implemented, verified and user-accepted status, evidence, blockers and next tasks. Commit and push small validated milestones and verify the remote hash. Preserve saves; never claim uncommitted work is backed up. Respect usage/approval limits. Complete the actual acceptance gates rather than treating a short visual pass as the whole goal.
